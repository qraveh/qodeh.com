Single-flux-quantum logic — RSFQ, ERSFQ/eSFQ, RQL, AQFP and DSFQ, treated as one field — is a genuine outlier. It holds the outright digital speed record, a 770 GHz toggle flip-flop from 1999 that no technology has beaten, and it switches at roughly 2×10⁻¹⁹ J, about 1,000× below CMOS before the cryogenic cooling tax and a conditional 10–30× after it.

It is also, stated plainly: not dense — some 10³–10⁵× behind CMOS; memory-poor, with a 4 K RAM record of 64 kb unbeaten since 2013; and tool-poor, with no commercial EDA flow.

Those facts sit scattered across physics venues, company filings and program documents. SFQ falls in the seam between quantum reports, where it appears as a footnote, and semiconductor reports, where it does not appear at all. This Monitor consolidates them into a single picture:

- a **subsystem scoreboard** where every load-bearing figure carries a confidence tier (T1 demonstrated-primary through T4 modeled-estimate) and a per-subsystem readiness level, never blended;
- a **2026 ecosystem census** — seven disclosed fabrication lines, three disclosed SFQ-first venture companies totalling ~$110M, and the $1.8B IonQ–SkyWater acquisition that closed on 31 July 2026;
- **market scenarios to 2035** — bear $0.5–1.5B, base $3–8B, bull $20–35B, scenario-weighted mean ≈$6.5B.

Quantum-computer control is the pivotal near-term market. SFQ is the only logic family with demonstrated qubit-control waveform synthesis at millikelvin, reaching up to 99.9% single-qubit fidelity in 2026 — physics-favored, though not physics-mandated, against cryo-CMOS at the 2–23 mW/qubit demonstrated today.
